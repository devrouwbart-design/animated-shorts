# The King Who Taxed Shadows

Original ~60 s cartoon 3D short. The story is invented (a fairy-tale kingdom, our own characters); the science in it
is real. Narrator: ElevenLabs "Henry – Deep, Professional, and Soothing" (eleven_v4). One-word subtitles.

## Science checked (every claim the script makes)
| # | Claim in the script | Why it is true |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shadows are shortest at noon in midsummer, when the sun is highest. | Shadow length = height / tan(sun elevation). The sun's daily peak is at solar noon, and its yearly peak is at the summer solstice. Example at 50° N (a typical "medieval Europe" latitude): midsummer noon elevation = 90 − 50 + 23.4 = 63.4°, so a shadow is about 0.5x your height. |
| 2 | On a winter evening the sun hangs low and shadows stretch across the square. | Same formula: at 5° elevation a shadow is 11.4x your height (a 1.7 m villager casts ~19 m); at 2° it is ~29x. Winter suns are low all day at that latitude (max 16.6° at noon on the winter solstice) and lowest near sunset. |
| 3 | Torch straight down on a toy: shadow hides underneath. From the side: the shadow gets long. | The everyday version of claim 1 and 2: light from overhead throws the shadow straight down under the object; light at a low angle throws it far along the floor. |
| 4 | Thick clouds scatter sunlight across the whole sky, so light comes from everywhere at once; shadows go soft and faint with no edge to measure. | Under thick overcast there is no direct sunbeam: all light is diffuse, scattered by cloud droplets, arriving from the whole sky dome. A sharp shadow needs a small light source; a sky-sized source gives only a faint, edgeless darkening right under objects. The script says "soft and faint", not "gone". |
| 5 | Inside a shadow no sunlight reaches you, so you cast no shadow of your own. | A shadow is the region where the direct sunbeam is blocked. Inside the castle's shadow nobody receives direct sunlight, so nobody blocks any, so no one casts a shadow of their own (only soft skylight remains, as in claim 4). |
| 6 | The castle's shadow is the biggest in the kingdom and stretches over the town at sunset. | A tall building at a low sun angle: a 30 m keep at 2-5° elevation throws a shadow ~340-860 m long, longer than a small town square. |

Fiction, not claimed as fact: the king, the tax, the collector, the town.

Analogy: a torch shining on a toy from above vs. from the side.

## Script (v1, 214 words, ~60 s at Henry's natural pace)
| Beat | Line |
|---|---|
| Hook | A greedy king once made his people pay a tax on their shadows. |
| You see | You see, his collector measured every shadow with a long rod. The longer your shadow, the bigger your bill. |
| Round 1: noon | So the town got clever. Everyone paid at noon in midsummer, when the sun is highest and shadows are shortest. Tiny bills. The king was furious. |
| King strikes back + analogy | So he moved tax day to a winter evening. Now the sun hung low, and every shadow stretched right across the square. Shine a torch straight down on a toy, and its shadow hides underneath. Shine it from the side, and the shadow gets long. Huge bills. |
| Round 2: clouds | Next, the town waited for a cloudy day. Thick clouds scatter sunlight across the whole sky, so light comes from everywhere at once. Shadows go soft and faint, with no edge to measure. The collector measured nothing. |
| Mystery | Finally, the king picked a winter sunset without a single cloud. Now what? |
| The trick | The whole town squeezed into the long shadow of the king's own castle. Inside a shadow, no sunlight reaches you, so you cast no shadow of your own. Zero tax. |
| Twist | But the collector followed the rules. He measured the biggest shadow in the kingdom, the castle's, and handed the bill to the king. |
| End | The tax was gone by morning. |
