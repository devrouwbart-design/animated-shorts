A greedy king once made his people pay a tax on their shadows.
You see, his collector measured every shadow with a long rod. The longer your shadow, the bigger your bill.
So the town got clever. Everyone paid at noon in midsummer, when the sun is highest and shadows are shortest. Tiny bills. The king was furious.
So he moved tax day to a winter evening. Now the sun hung low, and every shadow stretched right across the square. Shine a torch straight down on a toy, and its shadow hides underneath. Shine it from the side, and the shadow gets long. Huge bills.
Next, the town waited for a cloudy day. Thick clouds scatter sunlight across the whole sky, so light comes from everywhere at once. Shadows go soft and faint, with no edge to measure. The collector measured nothing.
Finally, the king picked a winter sunset without a single cloud. Now what?
The whole town squeezed into the long shadow of the king's own castle. Inside a shadow, no sunlight reaches you, so you cast no shadow of your own. Zero tax.
But the collector followed the rules. He measured the biggest shadow in the kingdom, the castle's, and handed the bill to the king.
The tax was gone by morning.
