THE WAR WE LOST TO A BIRD - voice-over script
(about 230 words, aim for 60-65 seconds)

In 1932, Australia sent the army to fight a bird. And the bird won.

You see, after the First World War, Australia gives its old soldiers farms out west, to grow wheat. Hot sun, red dirt, and miles of golden wheat. Then twenty thousand emus walk in for dinner.

The farmers are soldiers, so they ask for soldiers. The army sends Major Meredith, two gunners, two machine guns, and ten thousand bullets.

First, it rains. Then the emus split up, and run out of range.

So the major sets an ambush at a dam. A thousand emus come to drink. He waits until they're a hundred yards away, and opens fire.

Twelve emus later, the gun jams.

Luckily, he has a plan B: a machine gun on a truck. Unluckily, emus run fifty kilometres an hour, and the ground is so bumpy nobody can aim.

Wait. How do you miss a six-foot bird?

Picture a garden hose. Spray it at a crowd standing still, and everyone gets wet. But these birds never stand still. Every single one runs a different way. The major said they could face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks.

After nearly ten thousand bullets, he claims nine hundred and eighty-six emus. That's ten bullets per bird. The army goes home, and the farmers get paid per emu instead.

Today, the emu stands on Australia's coat of arms. The army doesn't.


RECORDING TIPS
- One steady, calm storyteller tone the whole way, like the lake video. Let the jokes land by staying deadpan.
- Short pause after each paragraph. Slightly longer pauses after "And the bird won.", "the gun jams." and "How do you miss a six-foot bird?"
- Quiet room, phone or mic about a hand's width from your mouth.
