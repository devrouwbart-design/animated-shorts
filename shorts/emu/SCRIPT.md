# The War We Lost to a Bird

Original 55-70 s cartoon 3D short about the 1932 "Great Emu War" in Western Australia. Story mode, one steady narrator, no captions.

## Facts (with sources)
| # | Fact used in the script | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | After World War I, Western Australia gave ex-soldiers farmland in the wheatbelt; they grew wheat. | Britannica "Emu War"; Australian Geographic "Looking back: Australia's Emu Wars" |
| 2 | In 1932 about 20,000 emus moved into the farms around Campion (~170 miles east of Perth), eating and trampling the wheat. | Mental Floss "When Australia went to war with its emus"; Britannica |
| 3 | The farmers (veterans) asked the government for machine guns; the army sent Major G. P. W. Meredith of the Royal Australian Artillery, two soldiers, two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds. | Mental Floss; SOFREP; Wikipedia (via search) |
| 4 | Rain delayed the start and scattered the emus. | Wikipedia (via search) |
| 5 | First encounters: the birds split up and ran out of range. | Mental Floss; History Hit |
| 6 | Ambush at a dam with about 1,000 emus; Meredith held fire until ~100 yards; about 12 birds were killed, then the gun jammed and the rest escaped. | Mental Floss; Sandboxx |
| 7 | A Lewis gun was mounted on a truck; the rough ground made aiming nearly impossible and the truck could not keep up. | Mental Floss |
| 8 | Emus run up to ~50 km/h (30 mph) and stand up to ~1.9 m. | Australian Museum / general reference |
| 9 | Meredith: a division "with the bullet-carrying capacity of these birds ... would face any army in the world. They can face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks." (1932 press) | Modern Farmer; Scientific American blog; History Hit |
| 10 | Meredith claimed 986 emus killed using 9,860 rounds: ten rounds per bird. The army withdrew. | SOFREP; Mental Floss; Wikipedia (via search) |
| 11 | Later the farmers got a bounty system instead: 57,034 bounties claimed in six months of 1934. | Pickering Brook Heritage Group; ScienceBlog |
| 12 | The emu is one of the two animals on Australia's coat of arms (with the kangaroo). | Australian Government coat of arms (since 1908) |

Not used (sources disagree or unverified): the ornithologist's "guerrilla tactics" quote, exact kill numbers per day, the exact deployment dates.

Analogy: a machine gun is like a garden hose. Great against a crowd standing still. Useless when every target runs a different way at fifty kilometres an hour.

## Script v1 (~205 words, ~60 s at 3.5-3.7 words/s)
| Beat | Line |
|---|---|
| Hook | In 1932, Australia went to war against a bird. And the bird won. |
| Setting | After the First World War, Australia gave its old soldiers farms in the west, to grow wheat. Then twenty thousand emus walked in for dinner. |
| Action 1 | The farmers were soldiers, so they asked for soldiers. The army sent Major Meredith, two men, two machine guns, and ten thousand bullets. |
| Action 2 | First, it rained. Then the emus split up and ran out of range. |
| Action 3 | So the major set an ambush at a dam. A thousand emus came to drink. He waited until they were a hundred yards away, and opened fire. |
| Snap | Twelve emus later, the gun jammed. |
| Double line | Luckily, he had a plan B: a machine gun on a truck. Unluckily, emus run fifty kilometres an hour, over ground so bumpy nobody could aim. |
| Explanation | A machine gun is like a garden hose. Great on a crowd standing still. Useless when every bird runs a different way. The major said these birds could face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks. |
| Consequence | After nearly ten thousand bullets, he claimed nine hundred and eighty-six emus. Ten bullets per bird. The army went home. The farmers got paid per emu instead. |
| Twist | Today, the emu is on Australia's coat of arms. The army is not. |

## Picture notes (for the shot plan)
- World: flat red-earth wheatbelt under a big blue sky. Golden wheat fields with wire fences, a tin-roof farmhouse on stilts, a windmill water pump, a round farm dam, gum trees (Kenney nature kit, recoloured), a 1930s army truck.
- Cast (flat cartoon cards, our own designs): Major Meredith (moustache, slouch hat, shorts), two gunners, farmer veterans; emus as goofy cartoon cards (long neck, shaggy body, big eyes, strutting walk cycle), one tall "lookout" emu.
- Gags (all real): emus scattering in every direction from one gun; the gun jamming with a single sad "click"; the truck bouncing behind sprinting emus; the major's tally board "10 bullets = 1 emu"; final shot of the coat of arms with the emu proudly on it.
- Light: day for the war, golden for the army driving home and the twist.
